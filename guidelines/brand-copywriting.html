Copywriting

Clarity comes first. Warmth comes second. Brand expression comes third.

Shantara is a naturopathy retreat, not an abstract wellness brand. Public copy is written for a prospective guest. The visitor should understand Shantara without having to understand the website architecture, the CMS, or internal business rules. Full skill: Website language and copywriting. Internal audience strategy (who we are trying to reach) lives in ICP and marketing audience — do not paste those labels into public headings.

Say what the thing is

A visitor scanning only the headings should understand the structure and substance of the page. If a plain phrase describes something accurately, keep it plain.

Write for the visitor

Never explain how the website is organised. Do not mention hubs, listings, kits, category URLs, or where content lives.

This listing groups conditions visually. It does not create category URLs.Our doctors see guests with a range of long-standing conditions.
Rates and inclusions live on the tariff card, not on this hub.View tariffs
What happens next · numbered ticket steps · Confirmed after doctor reviewWe'll be in touch. A member of the team will call you…
This kit does not create a page per therapy.Delete the sentence. No replacement is required.

Use positive framing

Explain what Shantara does. Do not define the retreat by arguing with an imaginary visitor.

Meals are prescribed diet therapy, not a restaurant menu.Meals are planned as part of your programme based on your doctor's recommendations and dietary needs.
The protocol does the deciding; the guest only has to arrive.Your programme is planned by the clinical team following consultation and assessment.
Nothing here is ordered from a list.Your programme is planned after consultation and assessment.

Be specific

Don't let "holistic", "transformative", "rejuvenating", "restorative", "natural" or "wellness" carry the meaning when something concrete can be said.

Experience a transformative journey towards holistic wellbeing.Your programme is planned by our doctors based on your assessment, health history and goals.
Reconnect with your body's natural ability to heal.Your stay may combine doctor-led naturopathy, nutrition, physical activity, therapies and rest.

Inform before persuading

Answer the practical questions before making the case to book. Never hide useful information behind brand copy.

What is naturopathy?What programmes are available?Who are they for?What happens before I arrive?What happens during my stay?Who supervises my programme?What will I eat?What about my medication?How long should I stay?What is included in the tariff?Can someone accompany me?What happens after I leave?

Do not invent policy

Do not write a site-wide minimum stay unless it is confirmed for that programme. Do not say therapies can never be booked separately unless that is a verified commercial rule. Prefer typical duration, recommended duration, or duration advised following consultation. Do not turn an observation into an absolute.

Pricing

Individual rates appear only on approved tariff or pricing surfaces. Do not write "Deluxe Room from ₹8,500" on a programme or accommodation card. Do not explain the pricing governance rule to visitors. Use "View tariffs". Keep button labels short (about one action). Do not narrate internal process on the enquiry sidebar. Do not use Badge or static Tag as decorative pills next to actions.

Punctuation

Do not use em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma, or two sentences. Do not build headings as a title, a colon and a subtitle. Use "Detox Programme", not "Detox Programme: A complete reset". Colons remain fine in tables, form labels and utility interfaces.

Voice

A knowledgeable practitioner and an attentive hotelier: calm, clear, respectful, reassuring. "We" for Shantara, "you" for the guest. "Guest", not "client" or "patient", except where a clinical or regulatory context requires it. Short sentences, concrete nouns, plain-language explanations of medical terms.

Never

Metaphorical headings that need the paragraph below to explain themAbstract wellness or spiritual languageGeneric luxury languageRepeated "journey", "balance", "transformation", "healing", "reconnection"Personifying protocols, systems or the bodyInternal notes, CMS language, or Welnez migration instructions in public copy

Clinical claims

Describe what the programme involves, what doctors assess, what treatments may be recommended, how guests are monitored and what follow-up is provided.

Never state or imply a guest will be cured, reversed, healed or made medication-free. Where outcomes depend on the individual, say so. Our doctors review current medication as part of assessment. Any change is considered individually and only when medically appropriate. Never pre-empt a doctor's assessment.

The final test

If the styling, photography and supporting paragraph disappeared, would the heading still tell the visitor what this section is about?

If not, rewrite it. Then ask whether the sentence tells the visitor something useful about Shantara, or merely makes Shantara sound like a wellness brand. If it only creates atmosphere, cut it or make it specific. If it could have appeared in a CMS brief, remove it.

Taglines (Finest hospitality by nature, Nurture in nature, Blooming in nature, always beyond!) are brand devices for designated placements, not a vocabulary source. The site should read completely with every tagline removed.
