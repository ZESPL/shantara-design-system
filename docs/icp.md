## 2. Organising principle

ICPs are organised around **recognisable customer needs and purchase intent**, not around programme names.

**Care model** (how a stay works — not a slogan):

```text
Assessment
→ Personalised plan
→ Therapies and routines
→ Measurable reassessment
```

Discovery and purchase still follow need → content → consultation. The care model is why programme names stay catalogue: the plan is personalised after assessment.

```text
ICP
→ Need / problem
→ Search and discovery intent
→ Appropriate Shantara content
→ Programme consideration
→ Clinical assessment
→ Personalised programme / stay
→ Measurable reassessment
```

Not:

```text
Programme → ICP
```

Programme matching is **many-to-many**. A guest may discover through one need and be recommended a different programme after assessment. The current catalogue illustrates capability. It does not define the ICP system. If a programme is added, renamed, or withdrawn, update [Need states and programme areas](#5-need-states-and-programme-areas) and the relevant profile’s section M. Do not rebuild the ICP list around the catalogue.

Customer language is what people type, say, and expect to read: Google Search, landing pages, articles, FAQs, ads, social, and enquiry conversations.

**Never assume an internal ICP name is language customers search for.** Agencies must validate actual search behaviour in each country and language. Internal labels are not automatically SEO keywords, ad headlines, or website headings.

## 7. ICP 01 Weight & Metabolic Health

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| ID | ICP 01 |
| CRM key | `weight_metabolic` |
| Strategic role | {Core} {Provisional} |
| Validation | {Hypothesis} |
| Internal thought | “I need to get my health and weight back under control before this becomes worse.” Not public copy. |

### A. Definition

People concerned about excess weight, blood sugar, metabolic deterioration, or lifestyle-related health risk, and motivated to make a meaningful intervention rather than another short diet.

### B. Core job to be done

Interrupt a metabolic pattern they no longer trust themselves to change at home, under doctor guidance, for long enough that meals, movement, and rest can be practised together.

### C. Recognisable problems / needs

| Kind | Examples (not claims) |
| --- | --- |
| Symptoms | Weight that keeps returning, low energy, sugar cravings, breathlessness on exertion, waist-line change |
| Diagnosed conditions | Obesity, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, dyslipidaemia, metabolic syndrome, hypertension as a related risk factor |
| Functional problems | Clothes and mobility, difficulty walking or climbing stairs, sleep apnoea mentioned by a clinician, fear of medication escalation |
| Goals | Lose weight sustainably, improve blood-sugar control, reduce metabolic risk, understand food in a way that lasts after the stay |

Do not present any diagnosis as something Shantara “treats and finishes”. Management, support, and assessment are the honest frame.

### D. Trigger events

{Hypothesis} until enquiry notes or guest research say otherwise.

- A new reading (HbA1c, liver enzymes, lipids, blood pressure) that frightened them or their family
- A clinician increasing or adding medication
- A family event they want to be well for
- Repeated diet attempts that did not last
- A relative’s cardiac event or diabetes complication
- Being told they have fatty liver or prediabetes
- Pre-retirement or a long leave that finally makes a residential stay possible

### E. Desired outcomes

Guests typically **hope** for weight change they can keep, steadier energy, clearer numbers on follow-up tests, and a way of eating and moving they can continue. None of this is promised. Medication change is a clinical decision, never a marketing outcome.

### F. Primary fears and objections

- “Will I just regain it when I go home?”
- “Is this medically serious, or a spa with salad?”
- “Can I stay on my current diabetes or blood-pressure medication?”
- “How long do I actually need to be away from work and family?”
- Naturopathy credibility versus conventional endocrinology
- Privacy about weight and diagnosis
- Cost of a residential stay (point to the tariff card; do not quote figures)

### G. Purchase criteria

Doctor involvement; whether meals are planned rather than optional; typical duration for metabolic work; what happens after discharge; evidence that this is clinical rather than cosmetic; whether a companion can stay; logistics to Kozhikode. Rates only on the tariff surface.

### H. Buying roles

| Role | How it often shows up {Hypothesis} |
| --- | --- |
| Guest | The person whose weight or metabolic markers are the reason for the stay |
| Initiator | Often the guest; sometimes a spouse or adult child after a health scare |
| Decision-maker | Guest, or guest plus spouse when time and money are shared |
| Influencer | Treating physician, previous guest, family member with diabetes experience |
| Companion | Spouse who comes for support, not always for the same programme |

### I. Search and discovery behaviour

All examples {Seed terms requiring research}. Validate per country and language. Do not publish these as if they were ranked keywords.

**Problem-led hypotheses:** lose weight, can’t lose weight, belly fat, high blood sugar, prediabetes, fatty liver, high cholesterol, obese, overweight, lifestyle disease.

**Solution-led hypotheses:** weight loss retreat, diabetes retreat, naturopathy for diabetes, residential weight management, metabolic health programme.

**Question-led research:** how to reverse insulin resistance, can fatty liver be improved with diet, how long should a weight loss retreat be, can I stay on metformin during a naturopathy stay.

**Branded / comparison intent:** Shantara, Shantara diabetes, Shantara weight; named competitor retreats; “best diabetes retreat India” style queries.

**Required comparison path (GCC / West):** Ayurveda vs naturopathy is a **required medically reviewed explainer** — one article/guide inside existing Insights families, not a keyword to rank by becoming Ayurveda, and not an Ayurveda service page. Intercept Ayurveda and wellness-retreat intent honestly. See [Overlays — venue language](#venue-language-by-market-measured-20-sep-2026) and [skill-ia.md](../ui_kits/website/skill-ia.md).

Internal name “Weight & Metabolic Health” is **not** a keyword target.

### J. Search intent stages

| Stage | What they are doing | Typical next content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Notices a number, a photo, or a comment about weight or sugar | Calm education, not a hard sell |
| Problem research | Reads about diabetes, fatty liver, obesity | Condition pages and doctor answers |
| Solution research | Compares diet, medication, retreats, apps | How a residential stay actually works |
| Comparison | Retreat vs hospital vs spa vs another Kerala property | Doctors, method, eligibility, tariff link |
| High commercial intent | Looks at programmes, duration, inclusions | Programme page + Book a Consultation |
| Brand validation | Checks credentials, reviews, press | About, doctors, editorial policy, guest experience (not efficacy claims) |
| Consultation intent | Ready to talk | Enquiry form, WhatsApp as secondary |
| Booking intent | After clinical guidance | Assisted conversion; no fake urgency |

### K. Content requirements

Territories: insulin resistance in plain language; what a residential metabolic stay involves; medication during a stay; fatty liver education; weight and diabetes as connected, not identical; meals as part of the plan; what follow-up looks like; eligibility and who should not come. Separate education from selling. Do not build thin doorway pages for every synonym.

### L. Evidence required

Clinician review on condition pages; doctor credentials; treatment / protocol explanation; facility capability; safety / eligibility; patient experience of the stay (not of “curing diabetes”). Original Shantara data and annual outcomes data if they exist and are approved. Peer-reviewed external evidence where a physiological claim is made. Do not invent success rates.

### M. Programme associations

Indicative: Weight Management; Diabetes Reversal (handbook name approved for use; do not write that it guarantees reversal or cures diabetes); Detox; Complete Healing; medical Metabolic & Lifestyle Health; Heart & Circulatory Health for medically stable guests. A guest in this ICP may be placed on a different programme after assessment.

### N. Appropriate CTA

| Stage | CTA |
| --- | --- |
| Education | Read the guide / Meet our doctors |
| Programme consideration | Learn about the programme / View programmes |
| Ready to talk | Book a Consultation / Speak to our team |
| Avoid | Book now, Begin your transformation, guaranteed results language |

### O. Conversion objective

Qualified enquiry that can be clinically triaged. Secondary: consultation completed. Booking is downstream of assessment, not the first success metric.

### P. Exclusions / poor fit

Marketing poor-fit: crash-diet shoppers, cosmetic-only weight loss, spa slimming, people unwilling to change meals, anyone expecting a guaranteed number of kilograms. Clinical eligibility is handbook-wide (acute / critical conditions, age policy, doctor’s right to decline). Do not add ICP-specific medical exclusions here without a clinical source.

## 8. ICP 02 Pain, Mobility & Rehabilitation

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| ID | ICP 02 |
| CRM key | `pain_mobility` |
| Strategic role | {Core} {Provisional} |
| Validation | {Hypothesis} |
| Internal thought | “I want to reduce the impact this pain has on my life and move better again.” Not public copy. |

### A. Definition

People whose pain or loss of mobility is materially affecting daily life, and who are looking for structured rehabilitation or non-surgical supportive management where that is appropriate.

### B. Core job to be done

Get a supervised period of assessment, movement, rest, and therapies that might reduce the day-to-day tax of pain, without pretending surgery or medication is never needed.

### C. Recognisable problems / needs

| Kind | Examples (not claims) |
| --- | --- |
| Symptoms | Persistent pain, stiffness, numbness, poor sleep because of pain, swelling |
| Diagnosed conditions | Osteoarthritis, other arthritis, spondylosis, disc-related pain, frozen shoulder, sciatica, gout, fibromyalgia (as named by their clinician) |
| Functional problems | Cannot sit through work, cannot walk their usual distance, difficulty dressing or climbing stairs, fear of becoming dependent |
| Goals | Move with less fear, return to ordinary tasks, understand what to do at home, delay or complement surgical pathways where a doctor agrees |

Do not imply that Shantara replaces orthopaedic or rheumatology care. “Non-surgical” is a hope some guests bring; it is not a promise.

### D. Trigger events

{Hypothesis}

- Pain that has lasted months despite outpatient physiotherapy
- A scan result (disc, knee, shoulder) and a wish to try conservative care
- Surgery recommended, guest wants a second path or prehab
- Pain starting to limit work or care for family
- A flare after travel or a period of inactivity
- A previous retreat or hospital stay that helped temporarily

### E. Desired outcomes

Guests typically **hope** for less day-to-day pain, better range of movement, confidence to walk or work, and a plan they can continue. Pain reduction is not guaranteed. Imaging “fixing” is not a claim.

### F. Primary fears and objections

- “Will they make it worse?”
- “Is this real rehab or massage?”
- Time away versus an outpatient clinic near home
- Whether doctors understand their specific joint or spine issue
- Need to continue existing pain medication
- Accessibility of rooms, grounds, and treatment spaces
- Scepticism of naturopathy for structural problems

### G. Purchase criteria

Named clinical oversight; physiotherapy / movement competence if offered; typical duration for rehab; honesty about what cannot be treated; accessibility; companion policy; evidence of similar cases without promising the same result.

### H. Buying roles

| Role | How it often shows up {Hypothesis} |
| --- | --- |
| Guest | The person in pain |
| Initiator | Often an adult child or spouse for older guests; the guest themselves for working-age musculoskeletal pain |
| Decision-maker | Frequently shared with family when mobility and travel are hard |
| Influencer | Orthopaedist, physiotherapist, previous guest |
| Companion | Common; may be required for support. Companion is not automatically a guest on the same programme |

### I. Search and discovery behaviour

{Seed terms requiring research}

**Problem-led hypotheses:** chronic back pain, sciatica, frozen shoulder, knee pain, neck pain, arthritis pain, can’t walk far.

**Solution-led hypotheses:** back pain retreat, arthritis naturopathy, non-surgical disc treatment, residential physiotherapy, pain rehabilitation India.

**Question-led research:** can naturopathy help sciatica, how long is rehab for frozen shoulder, should I try a retreat before knee replacement.

**Branded / comparison intent:** Shantara arthritis, Shantara back pain; comparison with hospitals, Ayurvedic centres, and spa “pain relief” packages (the last is poor-fit traffic).

### J. Search intent stages

Awareness (pain becoming unignorable) → problem research (condition and region) → solution research (surgery vs conservative vs retreat) → comparison (clinical seriousness) → high commercial intent (programme / duration) → brand validation (doctors, facilities) → consultation → booking after assessment.

### K. Content requirements

Territories: living with chronic back or joint pain; what assessment looks like; movement and rest in a residential day; who is not a candidate; relationship to conventional care; accessibility; companion stays. Avoid miracle musculoskeletal before-and-afters.

### L. Evidence required

Clinician review; protocol explanation; facility capability (treatment rooms, movement spaces); safety / eligibility; doctor credentials; patient experience of function and stay quality, not of “curing arthritis”. External evidence if a mechanism is described. Original outcomes data only if collected and approved.

### M. Programme associations

Indicative: Inflammatory & Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation; Complete Healing; therapies a doctor may include (for example acupuncture or massage as part of a plan, not as a menu). Cardiac recovery for medically stable guests sits primarily with ICP 06 and handbook Heart & Circulatory Health, not automatically here.

### N. Appropriate CTA

Read what to expect; Meet our doctors; Book a Consultation. Do not use “Book your pain-free life”.

### O. Conversion objective

Qualified enquiry with enough context to triage musculoskeletal complexity. Consultation before any implied programme match.

### P. Exclusions / poor fit

Marketing poor-fit: massage-only seekers, emergency acute injury, people wanting a guaranteed alternative to indicated surgery. Handbook exclusions cover acute / critical care. Do not invent a list of untreatable joints without a clinical source.
